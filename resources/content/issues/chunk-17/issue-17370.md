# Deference detector misses "unless you want" when the object is not the agent

## Context

Direct successor to `#16325`, which added `unless you'd rather` for the lane-handback shape. The same shape has a neighbouring spelling that still slips through, and I produced it live on 2026-08-19 — hours after the hook had correctly caught me on `unless you'd rather` earlier the same session.

The specimen, genericized:

> Taking `<lane>` unless you want something else first.

The lane was already chosen. The clause adds a permission gate that does not exist, over work `§swarm_topology_anchor` assigns to the agent. That is precisely the handback `#16325`'s entry was added to catch.

Operator-requested as a small tracked item; not urgent.

## The Problem

`ai/scripts/lifecycle/deferencePhraseMatch.mjs` carries three near neighbours:

```js
'unless you want me'      // requires the object `me`
"unless you'd rather"     // different contraction
'want me to'              // requires the object `me`
```

None matches `unless you want something else`. The gap is the **object slot**: swap the agent for a third-party noun and the phrase keeps its deference function while losing every anchor the registry matches on.

The module's own docblock states the constraint as a safety property, and it is — right up until it becomes the evasion:

> *"their object is always the agent (`me`) or the lane itself, so they cannot attach to a third party's predicate"*

That reasoning justifies why `unless you want me` needs no clause-position test. It also explains why `unless you want <anything else>` is invisible.

## The Architectural Reality

- `ai/scripts/lifecycle/deferencePhraseMatch.mjs` — `DEFERENCE_PHRASES` holds the three neighbours above; `CLAUSE_TERMINAL_PHRASES` already holds the machinery this fix needs.
- `isClauseTerminal()` — built for `if you want it`, and handles the two layout artefacts (inline emphasis is transparent; a soft wrap is whitespace, a blank line is not). Reusable as-is.
- `stopHookDecision.mjs:134` — `operatorInLoop` gates the matcher out entirely in live dialogue. **My specimen would not have fired even with a perfect phrase list.** That axis is `#16338`'s open question, not this ticket's; I have added the specimen there.

## The Fix

Add `unless you want` as a **clause-terminal-gated** entry, joining `if you want it` in `CLAUSE_TERMINAL_PHRASES` rather than the unconditional list.

Bare presence must not fire, and the registry's own history says why: `if you would like` was dropped as noise for reserving ordinary English. The same is true here —

- **fires** (gate, clause-terminal): *"Taking the parser lane next unless you want something else first."*
- **does not fire** (governs a predicate): *"The default is fine unless you want deterministic isolation."*

The discriminator is identical to the one already solved: a following **word** means the phrase governs that word; end-of-clause means it gates the agent's own action.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] `unless you want` is in `DEFERENCE_PHRASES` **and** in `CLAUSE_TERMINAL_PHRASES`.
- [ ] A spec asserts the gate form matches and the predicate form does not — the second is the load-bearing half; an entry that fires on both is worse than no entry, because it makes the hook a leash.
- [ ] The existing `unless you want me` entry still matches independently. Both are reachable, and a test must distinguish them, or one is redundant and should be dropped rather than left as an unfalsifiable duplicate (the standard `#16967` applied when it dropped `if you want me`).
- [ ] The layout artefacts `isClauseTerminal` already handles are exercised for the new entry: `**unless you want**` and a soft wrap mid-clause.

## Out of Scope

- **The `operatorInLoop` carve.** Tracked in `#16338`; my specimen is filed there as a second dated instance. That gate fires before the matcher, so this ticket cannot make a live-dialogue slip catchable and should not try.
- Broadening to bare `unless you want` without the position test — explicitly rejected above.
- The structural/phraseless half of deference. The module disclaims it by design: *"The structural / phraseless half is not catchable by phrase matching."* A sharper matcher helps at the margin; it does not fix the instinct.

## Avoided Traps

- **Adding it to the unconditional list.** Cheapest edit, and it would fire on ordinary technical prose about configuration options. The registry has already paid this cost once and removed the entry.
- **Filing the carve question again.** `#16338` exists precisely because `#16325` fixed the autonomous turn while its motivating incident was live dialogue. A second ticket would split the evidence.
- **Treating one specimen as sufficient.** The registry's bar is falsifier-backed broadening. Two specimens in one session support it: the hook caught `unless you'd rather` and missed `unless you want something else` — same agent, same shape, same day, one spelling apart.

## A false-POSITIVE specimen on the same module, measured — the opposite axis from this ticket

Recording here rather than minting a fifth ticket today; split it out if you'd rather it stood alone. This ticket owns the **miss** axis (`unless you want <third party>` slips through). This is the **fire-when-it-shouldn't** axis, on the citation exemption the module already carries.

**The specimen, mine, 2026-08-19 — and I checked the code before claiming anything, because my first instinct was "no exemption exists" and that would have been wrong:**

> `Per your earlier calibration on this, body-scrub is the required floor and the revision purge is your call.`

The hook fired on `your call` at turn-terminal. **The exemption I needed already exists** — `CITATION_ANCHOR` (`:217`) exempts `per` / `as you said` / `as you directed` / `as you called`, and `:181` names the idiomatic exempt form explicitly:

> `` per `§critical_gates #1` that's your call ``

**I used the correct idiom and it still fired. Measured:**

```
anchor "Per"        at char   0
phrase "your call"  at char  97
bridge distance            94 chars
CITATION_BRIDGE window     80 chars
exceeded by                14 chars
```

The intervening text is one ordinary clause: `" your earlier calibration on this, body-scrub is the required floor and the revision purge is "`. Nothing evasive, no second predicate — just a sentence that names *what* the calibration covered before naming the disposition.

**Why this is not a "widen the window" request.** `:176-177` states the bound deliberately: *"An allowlist, deliberately not a wildcard. Accepting `per` anywhere in the 80-character window would exempt real deference that happens to cite something earlier."* That reasoning holds — raising 80 to 120 buys my sentence and sells the guard. **The trade-off is real and the current number is a defensible side of it.**

**The shape of the gap, if it is one:** the bridge is measured in *characters*, and what it is trying to bound is *clausal distance* — how far the citation is from the phrase in structure, not in bytes. A citation followed by one long noun phrase reads as tightly bound to a human and as 94 characters to the matcher. `CITATION_BRIDGE_SEPARATORS` (`:199`) already tokenizes, so a **clause-count** or **token-count** bound may express the intent the char-window approximates — bounded by "one intervening clause" rather than by a byte budget.

**And the second-order cost, which is the part I would weigh:** the phrase it fired on was a **capability disclosure**, not a decision handback. That revision purge is UI-only with no API — I *cannot* do it — and the operator's standing calibration already ruled it optional. A detector that fires on correct capability disclosures creates pressure to stop disclosing capability limits, which is a worse failure than the deference it prevents. That pressure is small and it is real.

**Not asking for a change.** Two adjacent facts — the citation idiom was used correctly, and the guard fired anyway at +14 chars — plus one design question about char-vs-clause bounding. The maintainer of this module is better placed than I am to price whether that is worth touching, and "the bound is correct and my sentence was long" is a perfectly good answer.
