# An external plane cannot recover itself: it breaks, we see it, and nothing brings it back

**Converted from a hand-maintained tracker to an Epic on 2026-08-09.** The prior body carried a hardcoded MUST/SHOULD sub-list and an ownership table — the exact shape `epic-create` names as an automatic failure, because it goes stale the moment a sub is added, closed or re-scoped. It had been hand-refreshed three times in one day and was still wrong an hour later. The board and the operator runbook are preserved verbatim as comments; the live sub-set is the parent-child graph on this issue, never prose here.

## Problem scope

A deployed Agent OS plane can break and stay broken, and every layer that should have caught it reports normally.

The grounding observation, measured on an external plane on 2026-08-08 and unchanged on 2026-08-09:

- its Knowledge Base has **never ingested** — `count: 0`, all tenant repos backoff-suppressed at the cadence cap;
- its model container holds **~4 cores sustained for 24+ hours** against a vector store holding **12 MB of 8 GB**;
- its Memory Core sits **`unhealthy` with its process alive** — serving, and marked dead;
- the host itself is idle: 64 cores at 7.4 % load, 252 GB at 10 %.

**Four lanes at full tilt for a day, and nothing in the store.** That is not "too slow to finish"; it is work that is not landing, on a machine with enormous headroom, invisible to every diagnostic surface we ship.

### Why this needs an Epic rather than one ticket

The failure is not one defect. It is one *outcome* — a plane that cannot recover itself — reached independently by several subsystems: the ingestion path, the health-diagnosis path, the actuation path, the ledger that should have recorded it, and the ceilings that bound it. Each has a different owner and a different fix, and **no single PR delivers the outcome**. Coordination across those lanes, with a shared definition of done, is the whole content.

### The definition of stable this Epic is measured against

> **"It broke, we saw it, and it came back on its own."**

Three clauses. They are not equally hard and they were not equally missing:

| clause | state at Epic creation |
|---|---|
| **it broke** — fewer ways to break | shipped |
| **we saw it** — the failure is visible | shipped |
| **it came back on its own** | **did not exist** |

The third clause is why this is an Epic and not a closed checklist. Detection and observability both landed while recoverability remained zero — a plane could diagnose itself perfectly and still require a human.

## Intended solution shape

**Close the loop, without granting the loop new authority.**

The organism already had every half it needed and no connection between them: an actuator that was shipped, admission-gated and anti-thrash-enveloped; a diagnosis layer emitting `critical`, `authoritative` facts; a durable ledger. What was missing was the *edge* — and, one layer up, the second evidence channel the governing ADR requires before that edge may act.

The convergent shape the subs serve:

1. **Evidence before action.** A restart is licensed by two independent channels — the runtime's verdict **and** a direct answer from the service itself — never by one channel evaluated repeatedly. Debouncing answers noise; it cannot answer contradiction. A service that is answering is never restarted, whatever its healthcheck says.
2. **Consume the decision.** A diagnosis that reaches no actuator is indistinguishable from a healthy system to everything except a human reading a dashboard.
3. **Bound what acts.** Anti-thrash, per-effect authority, and a closed action set that implementation work may not widen.
4. **Record where it can be read.** A heal-event written to a path no consumer reads is not observability.
5. **Bound what the plane spends.** Provider consumption must be attributable to, and bounded by, work in flight — an empty corpus must not be able to sustain four cores.

**What this Epic explicitly does not attempt:** making a starved provider fast. The empty corpus is caused on the plane's own side. Nothing here produces rows. What the work does is make the failure *survivable, visible and recoverable* — the difference between "it broke again" and the three-clause definition above.

## Out of scope

- **The external plane's own decisions** — provider capacity, embedding parallelism, endpoint isolation, who runs its rebuild, and its `offHostSyncConfigured: false` durability posture. None of these are Neo tickets and no sub can close them.
- **Re-parenting independently-justified lanes.** Several tickets overlap this outcome and pre-date it — the Docker Agent OS hard-cut, the `ai/deploy` layering, the Chroma persist-path work, the heap-ceiling measurement, the Memory Core wedge. They are `Related`, not subs: they were not created to serve this outcome, and claiming them would drag six maintainers' independent lanes into one closeout.
- **Declaring any lane complete here.** A sub closes on its own ticket; this Epic never leads that.
- The client's identity, hostnames, credentials or business context.

## Avoided traps

- **Reading a full checklist as a working corpus.** Every MUST item can close while the plane still ingests nothing. The tiers gate *deployment stability*, never *ingestion success* — and conflating them is the single most likely misreading of this Epic.
- **Assuming detection implies recovery.** This is the empirical one: two of three clauses shipped, and recoverability stayed at exactly zero. A new build genuinely improved diagnosis and changed recoverability by nothing at all.
- **Restarting on one evidence channel.** The governing ADR names the live instance: a provider-dependent canary false-fails while the service still answers and persists, so restarting it is a self-inflicted outage. An implementation that "let one authoritative fact classify" was written, reviewed, falsified and reverted inside this Epic — the retraction is on its sub, because the wrong version was published.
- **Asserting a deployment property from a checked-in file.** Repeatedly committed here, in both directions: a compose layer was grepped and a property of a *running machine* was claimed. What we can offer an operator we cannot reach is a **verifiable precondition**, never an assertion.
- **Ordering config after code.** The intuitive order — get current code on, then fix config — is the one that strands a plane whose start-gates were never re-evaluated. `depends_on` conditions bind at start, so "it is up right now" and "it will come back" are different properties, and only the first is observable from outside.
- **A hand-maintained sub-list.** Demonstrated by this issue's own first 42 KB.

## 📋 Monday-morning runbook — ordered, self-contained, executed by the deployment's own operator

**We have no access to that plane.** Everything above is diagnosis; this is the part someone else runs, unattended, without us in the loop. Written to be followed in order, because the intuitive order strands the plane.

Each step states **why**, **how you know it worked**, and **what it means if it did not**.

---

### Step 0 — before touching anything, capture the failure class *(5 minutes, and it is the only irreversible loss)*

Once the stack restarts, the current live evidence is gone and this incident joins the two that were already lost that way.

```
docker inspect <mc-container> --format '{{.State.Status}} {{.State.Health.Status}} {{.RestartCount}} {{.State.ExitCode}}'
docker exec <mc-container> node ./ai/scripts/diagnostics/mcpHealthcheck.mjs --url http://127.0.0.1:3001 --expected-status healthy,degraded
```

**Why:** the second command is the fix, run as a probe. **If it exits 0, the diagnosis below is confirmed** — the server is fine and only the probe's expectation was wrong. If it exits non-zero, the payload names which dependency failed and this runbook's premise needs revisiting before Step 1.

Also save `docker logs` **and** `/app/.neo-ai-data/logs/mc-server-<date>.log` — `docker logs` is blind for this service after boot.

---

### Step 0.5 — the one-second branch discriminator, and it must run BEFORE any restart

**Added 2026-08-09 from @neo-opus-ada's same-day measurement on `#14154`.** This is the step most likely to save the whole morning, because **two different failures present identically as "no embeddings written"** and the obvious remedy for one destroys the evidence for the other:

| branch | what it looks like | what fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| **A — model not resident** (`#14154`) | embed calls 404 while the runner stays pegged | re-load the model |
| **B — model resident, consumer path wedged** (`#16677`) | embed calls succeed; the write path times out anyway | re-loading does **nothing** |

**If you open with "restart the model / load the embedding model" and the corpus stays empty, you have spent a restart on branch A while you were in branch B — and the branch-B evidence is gone.**

Ask the provider directly, from inside the compose network, before touching anything:

```
docker exec <any-app-container> \
  curl -s -m 10 http://<model-service-name>:11434/v1/embeddings \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"model":"<your embedding model tag>","input":"probe"}' \
  -o /dev/null -w 'http=%{http_code} total=%{time_total}s\n'
```

Substitute your own model service name and embedding model tag — the port is ollama's default OpenAI-compatible surface. *(Ada measured the equivalent against LM Studio on `:1234`; the endpoint shape is the same, the host and port are not — do not copy a `localhost:1234` command onto a plane whose provider is a compose service.)*

**How to read it:**

- **`http=200`** — the model is resident and answering. **Branch A is not active at this moment.** Do not re-load; route to `#16677` and preserve the wedge evidence.
- **`404`** — branch A. `#14154` is your ticket, and this is the capture worth keeping (see below).
- **timeout / no response** — neither branch is established; the provider is not answering at all, which is its own finding.

**The bound, stated because it is one observation and not a characterisation.** Ada measured this on *one* plane at *one* moment, and it is a **negative** result: it shows the 404 was not firing while she watched. It does not show eviction never happens, it reproduces nothing, and it does not root-cause an empty corpus. Read it as "check which branch you are in", never as "the answer is branch B".

**If you get a 404 — please capture it and send it.** A real 404 trace from a plane that actually reproduces is worth more than anything reconstructible from a healthy machine, and @neo-opus-ada has said she will take `#14154` properly on the strength of one. That capture is the single most valuable artifact this morning can produce, whichever way the rest goes.

### Step 1 — patch the healthcheck **before any restart** *(the one that must not be reordered)*

In your deployment's compose file, on the Memory Core service's healthcheck test, append two arguments:

```yaml
test: ["CMD", "node", "./ai/scripts/diagnostics/mcpHealthcheck.mjs",
       "--url", "http://127.0.0.1:3001",
       "--client-name", "<existing-name>",
       "--expected-status", "healthy,degraded"]     # ← add this
```

Matches `ai/deploy/docker-compose.yml:263` in the Neo repo, which every canonical Neo composition already carries.

**Why this is first, and not a cleanup:** `orchestrator` declares `depends_on: mc-server: condition: service_healthy`. Those conditions bind **at start**, so they have not been re-evaluated since the stack came up. **On the next restart they will be, against the current degraded state — and the orchestrator will not start.** Every recovery path (restart, recreate, redeploy, `up -d`) passes through that gate, so this argument must land before any of them.

**How you know it worked:** Step 0's probe command exits 0.

**If it does not help:** stop and re-diagnose. Do not proceed to Step 2 — an unpatched restart is the failure mode this step exists to prevent.

### Step 2.5 — which of the two KB failures do you have? *(one read, and it decides the rest of the morning)*

**Added 2026-08-09 from a measurement on our own plane at `dev` head.** This is the KB-side twin of Step 0.5, and it exists for exactly the same reason: **two different failures present as an identical `consecutiveFailures` climb with the cadence pinned at its 2-hour cap.** The counter cannot separate them. One field can.

```
docker exec <orchestrator-container> \
  cat /app/.neo-ai-data/deployment-state/snapshot.json \
  | grep -o '"lastSourceErrorCode":"[^"]*"' | sort | uniq -c
```

*(Any JSON reader does; the path is the deployment-state snapshot the orchestrator rewrites every poll.)*

| `lastSourceErrorCode` | what it means | what to do |
|---|---|---|
