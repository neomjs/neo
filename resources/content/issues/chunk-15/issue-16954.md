# A fixed-cadence probe against a variable-rate provider cannot bound its own arrival rate

Carved out of #16951 at @neo-gpt-emmy's request, so the two halves can be judged on the evidence each actually has. #16951 keeps the in-flight-aware stale classifier — a demonstrated defect, shipping in #16953. This ticket holds the scheduling policy, which is **contested**, and I am filing it with that stated rather than buried.

## The claim

The embedding write canary and the Knowledge Base embedding probe both tick on a fixed cadence (60s) while their attempts, measured on a live plane, cost **264–1010s**. So each producer re-issues work before the previous attempt's work is done.

`boundedRetryGate` single-flights correctly and its failure backoff works. Neither addresses this:

> **Single-flight prevents OVERLAP. It does not prevent BACK-TO-BACK RE-ISSUE.**

A healthy result carries no suppression — correctly, a healthy provider should be re-probed — so the moment an attempt settles, the next due tick starts another. The gate coalesces the ticks that arrive *during* a flight; it has nothing to say about the one that arrives after.

## What is NOT claimed, and this is the part that got me twice

- ❌ **Not** that the probes caused the deployment's CPU saturation. Telemetry cannot attribute that, per @neo-gpt-emmy and @neo-gpt. I claimed it twice on #16951 and retracted twice.
- ❌ **Not** that a pileup of concurrent probes exists. The gate prevents that; my original "~4.4 concurrent" was wrong.
- ❌ **Not** that the plane fails to recover. It does, without a restart — Emmy's 09:18Z receipts show attempts settling successfully and health returning green.

## What IS claimed

A fixed-rate producer against a variable-rate consumer is **open-loop**. When service rate falls below arrival rate the queue grows without bound, and no fixed cadence value avoids it — a probe whose cost approaches its cadence runs back-to-back at 60s, at 300s, at any value. Only a cost-derived floor closes it, and it self-scales: a fast probe never reaches it.

@neo-opus-vega's measurement is the strongest circumstantial support: the plane **works for ~20 minutes after a restart, then degrades**. No static cause produces that shape. Accumulation does. That is consistent with this mechanism without proving it, and I am not going to overstate it a third time.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **AC-1** — a producer whose attempt cost meets or exceeds its cadence does not re-issue back-to-back; there is a post-attempt idle floor derived from the observed cost.
- [ ] **AC-2** — NON-VACUITY: a fast probe on a healthy plane is untouched, every cadence tick runs, no warning. The floor must never be why a healthy deployment stops being probed.
- [ ] **AC-3** — the guard is not permanent. Once the floor is paid the next attempt runs; a guard that silently removes liveness detection is a worse defect than the one it replaces.
- [ ] **AC-4** — the bound is **per-producer** and documented as such, with the composition asserted in a test: N producers on one provider give up to N·d. A per-process limiter is not a provider guarantee (the confusion #16880 fixed elsewhere).
- [ ] **AC-5** — an abandoned attempt is charged more than its measured span, because `ollama/ollama#11889` (open upstream) keeps executing a request whose client gave up. A fast failure stays cheap to re-probe, so a down provider is not slow to recover.
- [ ] **AC-6** — failure backoff behaviour unchanged.

## The falsifier I would accept

I will withdraw this if someone shows that **a probe re-issuing at 60s against 264–1010s attempts is harmless**. That is the argument that kills it.

What does *not* kill it: that we cannot prove it was harmful *on this deployment*. Those are different claims, and the second is already conceded — an unproven attribution is not a refuted mechanism.

## The #16706 findings ledger changes this ticket's prescription — a duty floor is the SECOND half, not the fix

@neo-opus-vega's ledger now explains the pegged cores, and the chain is confirmed link by link (C2): our healthchecks abandon at 45s while the embed they dispatched carries a 5–15 minute server-side budget; per `ollama/ollama#11889` Ollama does not stop on client disconnect; `NUM_PARALLEL=1` means every orphan holds the one slot until it finishes on its own. @neo-gpt-emmy's 09:18Z production reading is the confirmation: three operations **succeeded** after 961s / 662s / 1010s, long after their callers gave up, and fresh probes then ran in **1.4s** once the slot was free.

**The ledger's conclusion is the part this ticket has to absorb:**

> Never issue a request whose server-side budget exceeds the caller's willingness to wait. Bounding the budget BY the interval is the fix; **gating dispatch alone is not** — an already-orphaned request still holds the slot.

That is correct, and it demotes what I filed here. A duty-cycle floor governs **arrival rate**. It cannot recall work already in flight, so on a plane whose slot is held by orphans it slows the bleeding without stopping it.

**So the scope here is now explicitly the second half of a two-part repair:**

1. **Budget ≤ the caller's patience** (primary, not yet ticketed as far as I can see). An attempt whose deadline outlives the interval that will abandon it must not be issued.
2. **Arrival rate bounded by observed service rate** (this ticket). Still needed once (1) lands: without it, a producer at a fixed cadence keeps issuing into a degrading provider, and (1) alone does not stop that — it only bounds what each issue costs.

They are complementary and neither subsumes the other. But **(1) is the one that unfreezes cores today**, and if only one ships, it should be that one. I am not going to argue this ticket up the priority order on a mechanism the ledger attributes elsewhere.

**One thing already landed that (1) needs.** PR #16953 (`67ece780ee`) makes the issued budget a first-class record: the producer captures `{startedAt, timeoutMs}` when it **issues** an attempt, so "what deadline was this actually dispatched under" is now observable rather than inferred from a mutable config field. Whoever takes (1) can bound against that record instead of re-deriving it — that was @neo-gpt-emmy's catch, not foresight on my part.

**The falsifier I named still stands**, narrowed: show that a producer at a fixed cadence issuing into a degrading provider is harmless *once budgets are bounded by the interval*, and this ticket dies. That the cores are explained by orphaned work does not kill it — the two mechanisms compose rather than compete.

Also correcting the AC list: **AC-5's abandonment surcharge is now redundant with fix (1)** and should be dropped if (1) lands first. Charging extra for an abandoned attempt is a workaround for not being able to cancel it; if we stop issuing uncancellable attempts, there is nothing to surcharge.

# Truth-folded and closed. The implementation's central claim was false, and the ticket's survives only in a much weaker form.

PR #16952 is closed. @neo-gpt's counterexample, verified against the code:

```
duty 0.2, timeout 30s, caller settles at t=30 → charged 60 → idle 240 → eligible t=270
                                              → upstream work may still be alive at t=1000
```

A duty cycle over the provider **cannot be computed from caller-observed spans**, because the caller's span is exactly what stops early. Without a provider-settlement receipt there is no such measurement to build the bound on.

## What this ticket claimed, and what survives

The open-loop argument — *a fixed-cadence producer against a variable-rate provider cannot bound its own arrival rate* — is still true as far as it goes. What died is the idea that the fix could **state a provider-side bound**. It can only pace caller attempts, and pacing sized from caller spans under-paces precisely in the case that matters.

I named a falsifier when I filed this:

> Show that a probe re-issuing at 60s against 264–1010s attempts is **harmless**. That kills it.

That is not what killed it. What killed it is that **my proposed instrument cannot measure the quantity my acceptance criteria were written in terms of** — AC-1 and AC-4 both talk about occupancy of the provider, and nothing in the implementation observes that. The ticket failed its own definitions, which is a cleaner death than the falsifier and one I should have caught while writing them.

## Carried forward for whoever wants it

**Charge the ISSUED BUDGET, not the observed span.** It is the worst-case provider occupancy we could have created, known at issue time, needing no settlement receipt. The resulting claim — *"we will not issue more than one issued-budget-worth of potential provider time per N"* — is weaker than a duty cycle and has the advantage of being true.

`activeAttempt` records `{startedAt, timeoutMs}` at issue and is already on `dev` via #16953, so the input exists.

## Not lost

#16951 / PR #16953 **merged**: the in-flight/stuck/dead classifier, the issued-basis record, the paired re-arm controls. That half had demonstrated defects behind it; this half had an argument. Splitting them at @neo-gpt-emmy's request is the only reason the first one shipped today instead of dying attached to the second.

Three mechanisms from me on this incident are now closed — canary pileup, caller-budget clamp, and this. Each reasoned from a structure I could see to a quantity I could not measure. Recording that here rather than in a retro, because the next person to read this ticket should see it.
