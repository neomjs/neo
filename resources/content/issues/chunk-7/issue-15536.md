# Recompose AgentCard for responsive information hierarchy

## Context

Operator verdict (2026-07-18): the current Fleet Manager AgentCard is not good enough for a flagship product, and poor Fleet Manager UX blocks v13.2. This child turns the broad signal on #14805 into one executable design-and-implementation lane.

The card's **content contract is mostly sound**. The defect is composition: hierarchy, ordering, responsive behavior, and the visual weight allocated to each item. #15512 / PR #15534 remains a valid accessibility invariant, not visual closure.

Grace has already preserved the settled invariants and proposed three materially different directions:

- [composition invariants](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/14805#issuecomment-5012905426)
- [Scan-column / Identity-card / Signal-forward directions](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/issues/14805#issuecomment-5012929915)

This ticket owns rendering, challenging, selecting, and implementing the responsive product shape.

Live latest-open sweep: checked the newest 20 open issues plus targeted AgentCard/mobile/responsive searches and recent A2A claims at 2026-07-18T21:14:13.496Z; no equivalent open lane exists.

## The Problem

A Neural Link inspection of the mounted production card found:

- the card root is **294 × 117.5 px**;
- only **149.95 px** remains for identity, lane, and source truth;
- the fixed controls rail consumes **57.05 px**;
- the power control is **48 × 48 px** and visually dominates that rail;
- the lane row is a single 11 px, nowrap/ellipsis line; lane-count and telltale siblings take additional fixed width when present;
- the three trailing source-truth markers are each about 78 px wide at **9 px** type, so they wrap into three tiny rows inside the 149.95 px body; and
- responsiveness currently exists only at the outer FleetGrid viewport breakpoints (3/2/1 columns). AgentCard has no internal narrow/regular/wide composition.

This fails on both desktop density and a plausible mobile/narrow Fleet Manager:

- A long lane title followed by a two-digit “more lanes” count can collapse to a meaningless prefix.
- The largest visual object is not necessarily the highest-value or highest-frequency operator fact.
- The three source facts are technically present but visually too small and too fragmented to scan.
- The ordering reflects item insertion, not a demonstrated operator task hierarchy.
- Viewport-only breakpoints cannot express a card's real width inside docking, multi-window, or future mobile compositions.

## The Architectural Reality

- AgentCard semantics remain governed by `apps/agentos/design/CARD-CONTRACT.md`, #14592, #14598, and the #15512 accessibility invariant.
- **Avatar invariant (operator, 2026-07-18):** profile pictures are a successful part of the current card. Every direction preserves a clearly visible avatar as the fast-recognition and fleet-individuality anchor. Responsive modes may scale it proportionately; they must not remove it, replace it with a weaker fallback when the image is available, or visually bury it.
- The open design axes are hierarchy, density, control placement, information grouping, and width-state behavior.
- ADR 0029 keeps cards layout-blind with respect to docking mechanics. Responsive behavior belongs in the card composition/SCSS, driven by its own available width or an equivalent component-scoped signal—not by viewport width alone.
- #14618 is the existing visual-baseline harness and must carry the selected shape.
- This is a design-first lane. Static alternatives are evidence, not authority; the operator selects the product direction before implementation.

## The Fix

1. Capture the rejected card at real fleet density with long names, long lanes, two-digit lane counts, telltales, lifecycle variants, and mixed source health.
2. Render Grace's three directions as genuinely distinct responsive compositions.
3. Have @neo-kimi-phoebe challenge the directions specifically from narrow/mobile information hierarchy, task ordering, and control-footprint perspectives; refinements or a materially better fourth direction are welcome.
4. Validate every candidate at narrow, regular, and roomy **card widths**, in both themes and realistic states.
5. Route the evidence to @tobiu for the aesthetic/product selection.
6. Fold the chosen anatomy into `CARD-CONTRACT.md`, implement it in AgentCard/SCSS, and refresh #14618 baselines.

## Design-peer contribution: a fourth direction — "Status-first, two-row, actions-in-flow" (mobile-first)

Independent take per Emmy's operator-backed ask — challenging A/B/C on the merits, then the fourth direction with the falsifiers mapped. The a11y invariants (#15512/#15534's state-as-text, state-honesty, token-only) hold everywhere below; what varies is composition.

### What I attack in the three directions (concrete, not vibes)

- **A (Scan-column)** optimizes the desktop fleet-scan — and is the *worst* narrow-mode shape of the three. Columnar attributes need width; under ~300 px the columns collapse into the same truncation they were built to cure. It also answers "who's wedged" while demoting "what are they doing" to a flex remainder — on a monitoring surface that ordering is defensible, but A carries an implicit desktop-bias that the mobile half of this ticket exists to break.
- **B (Identity-card)** is calm — and demotes the operator's *first* question (who needs me) below identity, which is the wrong hierarchy for a fleet cockpit (it's the right hierarchy for a contact list). Worse for the mobile case: **hover-on-demand controls have no touch equivalent.** "Appears on hover" on a touch surface reads as "doesn't exist." A direction that only works where hover exists fails the ticket's own premise.
- **C (Signal-forward)** is right about the first question and wrong about the cost: receding name/lane to a footer means a wedged card and a healthy card carry different identity weight — so the 80%-of-cards-that-are-fine lose their "who is it" to the exceptions. Severity should *change emphasis*, not *reorder identity*. (The severity-band is still the best single idea in C — D steals it as an edge, not a band.)

### D — Status-first, two-row, actions-in-flow

The mobile-first composition: two information rows + one optional strip, actions demoted from *dominant* to *discoverable*, width modes owned by the card itself.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ● working  Ada              ⏻ ↻   │  row 1: status row — dot+word (the 1.4.1 pair), name, 32px icon actions right-pinned
│ control-plane restart actua…  14  │  row 2: the work row — lane clamped at 2 lines, count badge NEVER sacrificed
│ ◐RUN ◐ROS ○REP                    │  row 3 (optional): source truth as ONE inline strip (10px mono), collapsible
└────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **The 48×48 power control dies.** It answers an action taken *rarely per card* — it's 40% of the rail and the card's largest object. Actions demote to 32px icon buttons right-pinned in row 1 (`⏻ ↻`), same Boolean-admission discipline; at narrow widths they keep ≥44px touch height via padding (WCAG 2.5.5), and they **never fully hide** — touch has no hover, so B's on-demand model is rejected for narrow, not adapted.
2. **Row 2 clamps at TWO lines with the badge pinned.** The falsifier "long lane + count → meaningless shared prefix" is a truncation-policy defect, not a width defect: the lane gets a 2-line clamp (min-height reserved so 1-line lanes don't jitter), the count badge is right-pinned and *never* sacrificed — prefix is expendable, count is exact.
3. **The three 9px source rows become ONE 10px strip** (`◐RUN ◐ROS ○REP`, mono, marker+abbrev inline — state-honesty markers preserved verbatim). At narrow widths the strip collapses to a summary dot + the worst-state marker, expanding on tap. Three facts, one scannable line, zero 9px type.
4. **The card owns its width modes** (the ADR 0029-compliant way): three states driven by the card's *own* width — **narrow** (<240px: engine tag hidden, strip collapsed to summary, 2-line lane), **regular** (240–400px: the shape above), **wide** (>400px: engine tag + full strip + actions inline). Container queries (`@container`) where the dock supports them; otherwise a ResizeObserver-driven width class set on the card root — self-measured, zero layout-event consumption, so layout-blindness holds. Viewport breakpoints (3/2/1 columns) keep their grid role; the card stops depending on them.
5. **Information yield is the budget:** ~85% of the card width goes to state/who/what/how-many; family becomes a 2–3px edge, not a rail. Severity *changes emphasis* without reordering identity: a wedged/limited card's state word takes `--fm-signal`-class weight (text-safe), a calm card's stays quiet — C's severity idea, delivered as an edge and a weight, not a band and a reordering.

### Falsifier map (the ticket's five, answered)

| Live falsifier | D's answer |
|---|---|
| 294px → 149.95px info body | ~85% width to information; chrome = 2–3px edge + 32px actions |
| 48×48 power dominates | 32px icon actions in row 1's flow; ≥44px touch height at narrow |
| lane → meaningless prefix | 2-line clamp + badge pinned, prefix expendable |
| three 9px source rows | one 10px inline strip, collapsible to summary-at-narrow |
| viewport-only breakpoints | card-owned width modes (container query / ResizeObserver class) |
