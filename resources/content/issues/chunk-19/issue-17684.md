# 29 of 40 ADRs never left Proposed, and a critical gate cites one that reads Draft

## Context

Surfaced 2026-08-24 when the operator challenged ADR 0040 §2.7 on `dev`. Reading my own record to answer, I found its `Status` still reading *"Proposed — 2026-08-22"* despite the merge that accepts it having landed. Checking whether that was a one-off found a class.

## The Problem

**40 ADRs in `learn/agentos/decisions/`. 12 read `Accepted`.** The other 28 read `Proposed` (25) or `Draft` (3), most carrying a transition condition satisfied long ago — ADR 0001 has said `Proposed — 2026-04-22` for four months.

> **Census corrected 2026-08-24 by doing the work, and the correction is itself the strongest argument for AC-3.** This ticket originally said *11 Accepted, 27.5% compliance*. Both numbers came from `grep '| **Status** |'` — a table-row pattern. **The corpus uses three syntaxes**, and each successive attempt of mine missed a different record:
>
> | syntax | count | missed by |
> |---|---|---|
> | `\| **Status** \| …` (table row) | 38 | — |
> | `**Status**: …` (colon outside bold) | 1 — ADR 0010 | the original grep |
> | `**Status:** …` (colon inside bold) | 1 — ADR 0031 | the first correction |
>
> ADR 0010 is `Accepted` and was counted as stale; ADR 0031 is `Proposed` and was counted as having no status at all. **A checker written against the shape its author happens to have open will report a confident wrong number** — which is precisely the failure this ticket exists to end, reproduced by the ticket's own first measurement.

The transition is defined but manual. A typical status field reads:

> `Proposed — 2026-08-22 (transitions to Accepted only on approved, green PR merge at the human merge gate, per ADR 0005)`

The condition is precise and checkable. Nothing checks it. `grep` across `buildScripts/` and `ai/scripts/lint/` finds no enforcement.

### The consequence, which is not cosmetic

**§critical_gates #10 reads: *"No AiConfig work without reading ADR-0019 first — no exception, no approval signal, no CI-green substitute."* ADR-0019's own `Status` field reads `Draft`.**

The strongest rule in the turn-loaded substrate points at a record that advertises itself as unsettled. An agent satisfying that gate opens 0019, sees `Draft`, and has to decide whether a draft binds them — with the gate's own text insisting it does. Same shape for the records governing live work right now: ADR 0039 (`Proposed`, cited for instrument semantics) and ADR 0040 (`Proposed`, governing Epic #17500's extraction topology). I cited both as authority repeatedly this week without noticing either says it is not yet accepted.

**A status field that is wrong in the safe-looking direction is worse than an absent one.** Absent, a reader asks. Reading `Proposed`, a reader gets a confident wrong answer and may treat binding authority as advisory.

### ADR 0005 predicted this exact failure and prescribed the remedy

`learn/agentos/decisions/0005-adr-at-graduation-for-ideation-sandbox.md:169`:

> **Compliance below 80%:** route to mechanical-enforcement automation ticket (CI check verifying ADR Status:Accepted on PRs citing `Decision Record: REQUIRED` Discussions)

**Measured compliance: 12/40 = 30%.** The trigger fired long ago; the escalation it prescribes was never routed. This ticket is that routing, three years of ADRs late.

## The Architectural Reality

- `learn/agentos/decisions/0005-*.md:169` — the compliance threshold and the prescribed remedy. This ticket executes an existing decision rather than proposing a new one.
- `learn/agentos/decisions/0005-*.md:26` — *"ADR = authority target … immutable post-Accepted"*. Immutability is keyed on a status nothing maintains, so the immutability contract is unenforced for 29 records.
- No lint, hook, or CI job reads the `Status` field. Verified by grep across `buildScripts/` and `ai/scripts/lint/`.
- Adjacent but not owning: **#13652** (@neo-opus-grace) mechanizes *behavioral* prompt-machinery — no-hold, wakes, cadence — and cites ADR-0019's lint as the shape; **#17481** covers ADR-0019's C1 rule having no enforcement. Neither covers decision-record status hygiene, and this should compose with both rather than widen either.

## The Fix

Two halves, and the second is what stops it recurring.

1. **Reconcile the 29.** Each record's status is a fact derivable from its own stated condition — the merge that resolved its `Resolves #N`. This is a census-and-correct pass, not a judgment call per record, *except* where a record's condition was genuinely never met, which must be recorded as still-Proposed with the outstanding condition named rather than swept.
2. **Mechanize the transition**, per ADR 0005's prescription. A check that a merged PR citing an ADR leaves that ADR's status consistent with its own stated condition. The precedent shape is ADR-0019's lint: the check replaces the discipline, because the discipline demonstrably did not hold for 29 records across four months and multiple authors.

**A recurring miss across independent authors indicts the template, not the authors.** Every one of us wrote a precise, checkable transition condition and none of us went back. That is a generator defect.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Every ADR's `Status` is consistent with its own stated transition condition, verified against the merge that resolved its close target — not assumed from age.
- [ ] Records whose condition was genuinely **not** met stay `Proposed` **and name the outstanding condition**; the pass must not convert unresolved into accepted for tidiness.
- [ ] A mechanical check fails when a merged PR leaves a cited ADR's status inconsistent with its stated condition.
- [ ] **The check parses all three Status syntaxes present in the corpus**, with one arm per syntax — table row, `**Status**:`, and `**Status:**`. Non-negotiable and separately asserted, because a status field the checker cannot read is indistinguishable from one it read and passed, and both of this ticket's own first two measurements failed exactly there.
- [ ] Either the corpus converges on ONE syntax, or the checker's multi-syntax support is documented as permanent. Silently tolerating three while parsing one is the current state.
- [ ] **NON-VACUITY:** the check is red on the current tree before the reconciliation lands — it must fail against today's 29, or it is not measuring what it claims.
- [ ] §critical_gates #10's target (ADR-0019) no longer reads `Draft`, or its outstanding condition is named so the gate and the record stop contradicting each other.
- [ ] The compliance figure is reproducible by command, so the next drift is measurable rather than rediscovered.

## Out of Scope

- **Re-litigating any ADR's content.** This is status-field accuracy only; a record that is wrong on the merits stays wrong and keeps its own successor path.
- ADR 0040 §2.7's `learn/agentos` custody disposition — reopened separately at [D#17489](https://github.com/neomjs/neo/discussions/17489#discussioncomment-18131998), different defect, same read.
- ADR-0019's C1 enforcement (#17481) and behavioral prompt-machinery (#13652).
- Changing the ADR template's status vocabulary or ADR 0005's process.

## Avoided Traps

- **Bulk-flipping everything to Accepted.** The 80% threshold is about *accuracy*, not about the field reading Accepted. A pass that marks unresolved records accepted would satisfy the count and destroy the signal.
- **Filing this as a docs chore.** It is cited-authority accuracy: a critical gate currently points at a `Draft`.
- **Widening an existing epic.** #13652 is the right philosophy and the wrong close target; composing beats absorbing.
- **Treating it as author negligence.** 29 records, many authors, one shared template with a manual step — the generator is the defect.

### The root cause, and it changes what the check must be

Most of these conditions read *"transitions to Accepted on approved, green PR merge."* **A record cannot appear on `dev` unless the PR that adds it merged.** So the condition is satisfied by *the same event that publishes the record* — it is true the instant the file exists.

**There is therefore no later moment at which anyone is prompted to act.** That is why 27 records drifted across four months and many independent authors: not one forgotten step repeated 27 times, but a condition whose satisfaction is simultaneous with publication and whose recording is not. Every author wrote a precise, checkable condition and every author was already correct when they wrote it.

**Consequence for AC-3:** the check must run **at merge time**, because merge time is the only moment that exists. A scheduled or post-hoc audit would flag 27 records forever without preventing the 28th; a merge-time gate is the only shape that closes the loop, and it is what ADR 0005 §169 asked for.
