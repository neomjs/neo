# Neo tickets required before an external plane's next deployment can be stable

## What this is

**The Neo tickets that must be resolved before an external plane's next deployment, for it to finally be stable.** Nothing else. Ownership stays where it is — this re-parents nothing, and each owner's ticket body is authoritative over the one-line summaries here, which will go stale.

Grounded in a live measurement of that plane on 2026-08-08: its Knowledge Base has **never ingested** (`count: 0`), all four tenant repos sit `backoff-suppressed` at `consecutiveFailures: 13` with cadence pinned to the 2 h cap, `accessReadiness` is **ready 4/4** (so not auth, not access), and a single embedding of a **three-word** string did not complete in **120 s**.

**Read this first, or the list misleads:** the empty corpus is caused on their side, not ours, and **no ticket below makes their provider produce rows.** What the tickets below do is make the failure survivable, visible, and recoverable — the difference between "it broke again" and "it broke, we saw it, and it came back on its own." That is what *stable* has to mean here.

**The root cause is refined as of 2026-08-09, and the refinement changes the fix class** *(supersedes this section's earlier "~399 % CPU, the embedder contending against a 26B" framing)*. Measured on their host at 01:26 Z:

```
64-core EPYC · load 4.42 (7.1 %) · 252 G RAM at 9.8 % used     ← the HOST is not starved
ollama runner · CPU-time 62:57:35 over 17 h  =  3.70 cores sustained, continuously
chroma        · 12.0 M resident of 8.00 G                      ← the store is empty
```

Per @tobiu, that ~400 % is **four parallel embedding lanes, not a four-core quota** — an earlier revision of this section inferred a throttle from the process table and was wrong. The surviving finding is stronger for it: **four lanes at full tilt for seventeen hours, and nothing in the store.** That is not *too slow to finish*; it is **work that is not landing**, which has a different fix than more hardware.

**A filed, idle ticket is the leading mechanism candidate: `#14154`** — *"KB-sync embedder 404s mid-sync ('resource could not be found' / model not resident) — root-cause the eviction."* If the embedding model is evicted to serve the chat model, embed calls 404 while the runner stays busy: four lanes, no rows, 100 % CPU. **Checking it costs nothing and should happen before any redeploy.** Embedding parallelism (1 vs 4) and shared-vs-isolated endpoints are a *tuning* comparison for after ingestion works, not a root cause.

## ⚖️ Deployment-readiness verdict — 2026-08-09

This ticket's title asks what is required before the next deployment can be stable. Measured answer, against this list's own three-clause definition of *stable*:

| clause | shipped? | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **it broke** — fewer ways | ✅ | `#16717` late-embed deferral · `#16692` sync rearm · `#16558` persisted ceilings |
| **we saw it** | ✅ | `#16691` KB health probes embedding · `#16690` outstanding-work depth · `#16724` `recentSlowCalls` |
| **it came back on its own** | ❌ | **does not exist** — `#16766` |

**A new SHA is a genuine improvement to visibility and survivability, and changes recoverability by zero.** Their Memory Core was observed `unhealthy`-with-process-alive at 01:26 Z, and nothing on the Neo side would have restarted it — not the container runtime (its `restart: unless-stopped` is exit-driven and the process had not exited), and not the immune system (`#16766`: diagnoses are recorded, never actuated).

**That plane is also the cleanest available control for `#16766`**: it declares `restart: unless-stopped` on six services *and* grants `NEO_ORCHESTRATOR_RUNTIME_ACCESS_LIFECYCLE_OPERATIONS=restart`. Policy declared, privilege granted, actuator instantiated — and an unhealthy service still never restarted. That eliminates missing-policy, missing-privilege and missing-actuator, leaving exactly one explanation.

**So the honest recommendation is: deploy the new SHA, and know that the next wedge is one you will diagnose faster and still have to fix by hand.** The list is not blocked on ticket inventory; it is blocked on the third clause having no implementation.

### ⚠️ A restart-ordering hazard to hand their operator — stated conditionally, because we cannot verify it

**Restated 2026-08-09, at the correct strength.** An earlier revision of this block read *"🛑 STOP — do not restart that stack; a cold start would not come back."* **That overstated what we can know**, and @neo-opus-vega — who raised the hazard — retracted it for the right reason before I could: we read `compose.yml` in a **local checkout** and asserted a property of a **running machine**. Those are different subjects, and *"is that server running this revision of that file"* is unverifiable from here by construction, since we have no access to it. Artifact verified, property claimed — the same defect that produced several corrections above, one layer over.

**It was also misdirected as urgency:** *"do not restart"* was addressed to people who cannot restart it. Nobody here operates that plane.

**What survives is a conditional, and it is worth handing over precisely because their operator CAN test it:**

> **If** the deployed composition matches the checked-in one, **then** the ordering below is mandatory. **They can settle that in one command on their host** — `docker compose config` renders the effective composition, including whether `orchestrator` gates on `mc-server: service_healthy` and whether the `mc-server` healthcheck carries `--expected-status`.

That is a better artifact than the warning was: an unverifiable assertion from us becomes a verifiable precondition for them. The facts below are facts **about the checked-in file** and nothing more.

```
kb-server     depends_on  chroma, local-model            condition: service_healthy
mc-server     depends_on  chroma, local-model            condition: service_healthy
orchestrator  depends_on  chroma, kb-server, mc-server   condition: service_healthy
```

**The orchestrator gates on `mc-server: service_healthy`.** That plane's mc-server healthcheck lacks `--expected-status healthy,degraded`, so while its embedding provider is saturated the server reports `degraded`, the probe rejects it, and the container never reaches `healthy` — **so the orchestrator never starts.** `kb-server` and `mc-server` additionally gate on `local-model: service_healthy`, so the saturated provider is on the critical path twice.

**Why a running plane is not evidence it would come back:** `depends_on` conditions bind at **start**, not continuously. Whatever gates applied when that stack came up were evaluated against its state then; the degradation arrived afterwards. Nothing re-evaluates them until something restarts — at which point they are judged against the state at *that* moment. **So "it is up right now" and "it will come back" are different properties**, and only the first is observable from outside. That reasoning is about `depends_on` semantics, not about their machine, so it holds independently of what is deployed.

**This is the failure `docker-compose.local-agent-os.yml:52` describes verbatim**: *"`ingress` + `orchestrator` gate on `service_healthy`, so the whole loopback plane fails to come up — taking a perfectly healthy Knowledge Base with it."* We hit it, fixed it in every canonical compose here, and documented it. That plane's compose never picked the argument up.

**Recommended order, conditional on the gate being present in their deployed composition (Step 0 of the runbook below settles it):**

1. **Patch the compose first** — add `--expected-status healthy,degraded` to the `mc-server` healthcheck test, matching `ai/deploy/docker-compose.yml:263`. **Nothing else is safe until this lands**, because every recovery path anyone would reach for — restart, recreate, redeploy, `up -d` — passes through those gates.
2. **Then** rebuild at a current SHA (their image predates all four MUST fixes, see above).
3. **Then** re-measure ingestion and attribute whatever remains.

**Doing 2 before 1 takes the plane down and it does not come back.** The intuitive order — get current code on, then fix config — is the one that strands it, which is the same shape as this list's original ordering trap and worth noticing twice.

*(The ingestion argument above still stands: diagnosing a 17-hour-old plane against today's `dev` mostly measures the gap between them. It is the **order** that was wrong, not the value of the redeploy.)*

## 📋 Monday-morning runbook — ordered, self-contained, executed by the deployment's own operator

**We have no access to that plane.** Everything above is diagnosis; this is the part someone else runs, unattended, without us in the loop. Written to be followed in order, because the intuitive order strands the plane.

Each step states **why**, **how you know it worked**, and **what it means if it did not**.

---

### Step 0 — before touching anything, capture the failure class *(5 minutes, and it is the only irreversible loss)*

Once the stack restarts, the current live evidence is gone and this incident joins the two that were already lost that way.

```
docker inspect <mc-container> --format '{{.State.Status}} {{.State.Health.Status}} {{.RestartCount}} {{.State.ExitCode}}'
docker exec <mc-container> node ./ai/scripts/diagnostics/mcpHealthcheck.mjs --url http://127.0.0.1:3001 --expected-status healthy,degraded
```

**Why:** the second command is the fix, run as a probe. **If it exits 0, the diagnosis below is confirmed** — the server is fine and only the probe's expectation was wrong. If it exits non-zero, the payload names which dependency failed and this runbook's premise needs revisiting before Step 1.

Also save `docker logs` **and** `/app/.neo-ai-data/logs/mc-server-<date>.log` — `docker logs` is blind for this service after boot.

---

### Step 1 — patch the healthcheck **before any restart** *(the one that must not be reordered)*

In `ai/docker-mcp/compose.yml`, the `mc-server` healthcheck test, append two arguments:

```yaml
test: ["CMD", "node", "./ai/scripts/diagnostics/mcpHealthcheck.mjs",
       "--url", "http://127.0.0.1:3001",
       "--client-name", "<existing-name>",
       "--expected-status", "healthy,degraded"]     # ← add this
```

Matches `ai/deploy/docker-compose.yml:263` in the Neo repo, which every canonical Neo composition already carries.

**Why this is first, and not a cleanup:** `orchestrator` declares `depends_on: mc-server: condition: service_healthy`. Those conditions bind **at start**, so they have not been re-evaluated since the stack came up. **On the next restart they will be, against the current degraded state — and the orchestrator will not start.** Every recovery path (restart, recreate, redeploy, `up -d`) passes through that gate, so this argument must land before any of them.

**How you know it worked:** Step 0's probe command exits 0.

**If it does not help:** stop and re-diagnose. Do not proceed to Step 2 — an unpatched restart is the failure mode this step exists to prevent.

---

### Step 2 — rebuild at a current Neo revision

```
export NEO_REVISION=<full 40-char sha from neomjs/neo dev>
```

**Why:** the running image is ~17 h old and predates all four MUST fixes on this list. `#16717` in particular — *a late embedding defers the ingest run instead of failing it* — directly addresses the observed `consecutiveFailures: 13` backoff-lock. Their build arg is `NEO_REF: ${NEO_REVISION:-dev}`, so pinning an explicit SHA also makes the deployed revision assertable rather than inferred.

**How you know it worked:** each container reports the expected SHA — `docker exec <container> cat /app/.neo-revision` — and all five reach `healthy`.

**If the plane does not come up:** Step 1 did not land. Check the rendered healthcheck with `docker inspect`, not the source file.

---

### Step 3 — re-measure ingestion before attributing anything

**Why:** diagnosing a 17-hour-old plane against today's `dev` mostly measures the gap between them. Several symptoms on this list may simply not survive Step 2.

**What to look for:** `consecutiveFailures` falling from 13 rather than resetting-then-climbing; the Chroma store growing past its current flat ~12 MB; `pendingDrainDepth` draining.

---

### Step 4 — only if the corpus is still empty: the provider, not us

**Check `#14154` first** — *KB-sync embedder 404s mid-sync ('resource could not be found' / model not resident)*. Filed, idle, and the leading candidate: if the embedding model is evicted to serve the chat model, embeds 404 while the runner stays pegged. That matches four lanes at full tilt for 17 h with a store that never grew.

**Then, and only then**, the tuning questions: embedding parallelism 1 vs 4, and a dedicated endpoint for embedding separate from chat. Both are post-ingestion comparisons, not root causes.
