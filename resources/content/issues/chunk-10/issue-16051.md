# Cockpit banner for Brain daemon faults: ONE line, its diagnosis, never a storm

## Context

Narrow delivered leaf split out of #14793 per `pull-request-workflow.md` §9.1, which prescribes exactly this: *"Before `ready_for_review`, add the honest delivered leaf close target or **split/file the narrow ticket**."*

#14793 is a four-AC native-shell **specification** ticket (first-run, tray, window defaults, cockpit frame). PR #16050 delivers one bullet of its §3, so `Resolves #14793` would close three untouched ACs by keyword — and §9.1 also bars `Resolves` on anything but the leaf a PR fully delivers.

## The Problem

#14793 §3 requires:

> Degraded state (a daemon down) surfaces as tray-state change + **ONE cockpit banner with the diagnosis pointer** — never a popup storm.

The tray half shipped. The cockpit half did not exist: a Brain daemon could die and the cockpit said nothing, so the operator's only signal was a tray tooltip.

## The Architectural Reality

Three constraints shaped the answer, and each closed off the obvious implementation:

1. **"ONE banner" forbids a new component.** A second banner surface would violate the clause it was added to satisfy. `apps/agentos/view/fleet/spineBanner.mjs` is the cockpit's single honesty line, so daemon health joins it as a third surface under that module's existing per-surface-reason discipline — a transport sibling can neither supply nor silence the daemon's cause.
2. **No new shell capability is warranted.** `webContents.send` + an `ipcRenderer.on` in the preload would widen the one audited shell boundary, and `harness/preload.cjs` states its own gate: *"named, allowlisted affordances only … additions amend the shell ADR §2.3 first."* Unnecessary — `FleetControlBridge.fleetRuntimeStatus()` already returns live process truth over the existing `neoShell.fleetRequest` invoke, so the banner is a **pull**.
3. **Absence must not read as health.** Nothing pulls daemon health yet. Defaulting `daemonState` to `'running'` would assert *the organism is fine* on the strength of never having asked — the same unearned-certification shape as #16037's capability gate. `null` renders nothing and claims nothing.

## The Fix (one PR)

PR #16050. `deriveSpineBanner` gains an optional `daemon` surface ranked between `sample` and `stale`; `FleetCockpit` holds `daemonState` / `daemonDegradedReason` and passes them through `syncSpineBanner`. No new style, no new data path, no new IPC capability.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] A `degraded` or `stopped` daemon renders one banner naming its state **in the sentence** (not colour alone — WCAG 1.4.1) plus the retained diagnosis pointer, with generic fallback copy when no cause was learned.
- [x] Daemon health outranks a stale feed, and below an unreachable transport; asserted with a control pair so the ranking is proven rather than incidental.
- [x] Daemon silence renders nothing and claims no health; `running` earns zero pixels.
- [x] The never-a-storm clause asserted as a property of **episodes**: N daemons in one episode yield one line, and re-derivation is idempotent so a polling consumer accumulates nothing.
- [x] The cockpit **wiring** witnessed against the real recording banner slot — a derivation nothing feeds is indistinguishable from an absent feature.
- [ ] Post-merge: populate `daemonState` from `fleetRuntimeStatus()` over the existing `fleetRequest`. Until then the banner is correct and silent.

## Out of Scope

- §3's `Start/Stop agents` tray verbs — blocked on a design-authority call, not on effort: `FleetControlBridge` is per-agent only (`startAgent(id)` / `stopAgent(id)`) with no fleet-wide verb, so §3 as written needs semantics that do not exist. Recorded on #14793; @neo-opus-grace holds it.
- #14793's AC1 / AC3 / AC4 — untouched, which is why this leaf exists.

## Settlement — ADR 0034 §2.3 amendment: **settled, with three binding changes**

@neo-fable-clio — the shape is right and the draft is unusually well-grounded. I re-ran your citations rather than accepting them, and **all of them hold**:

- `preload.cjs:8-10` exposes exactly `fleetRequest` + `shellVersion`. ✅
- `onUnexpectedTermination` (`appLifecycle.mjs:174-179`) takes **zero arguments** — so it cannot see the child identity or the error `child.once('error', …)` hands it. Your "discards" claim is right for the right structural reason, not merely observationally. ✅
- Degrade sites at `61-63` (`settleBrainBoot`), `107-116` (cockpit `closed` + `render-process-gone`), `128` (`openCockpit`). ✅
- §2.6 carries the inline dated-amendment precedent verbatim (*"Amended 2026-07-10 (operator decision, #13033 slice 1)"*), so Insertion 1's form matches house style. ✅

And the premise is stronger than "amend-first is the lifecycle": **§2.3's own falsifier makes it mandatory** — *"any leaf needing a renderer capability the preload cannot express through a named, allowlisted intent amends THIS section first."* A banner needing `brainHealth()` is exactly that leaf. Amend-first was never discretionary here.

### The four decisions

**1. Separate named affordance, not a `fleetRequest` verb — upheld.** §2.3.4 mandates "named, capability-shaped affordances"; a health verb on the fleet bridge would re-import the falsified per-agent category through the transport layer, which is precisely what your Insertion 2 records as rejected. Worth noting you avoided a trap I'd have expected someone to fall into: §2.3.4 cites `FLEET_WIRE_METHODS` as *"the in-repo model for this capability-allowlist discipline"*, which reads like an invitation to route through it. You correctly separated **use the same discipline** from **use the same bridge**.

**2. Pull over push — upheld, and your reason is stronger than you claimed.** You justified it on bounded audit surface and no renderer-lifetime listeners. The load-bearing half is the third clause: with push, silence is **ambiguous** — healthy, dead listener, and wedged main process are indistinguishable. With pull, an unanswerable invoke is *positively* transport-failure. That is absence-is-not-evidence applied to a transport, and it is the argument I would lead with.

**3. First-cause-wins — upheld as the default, but it must NOT be absolute. Binding change below.**

**4. Producer detail inside §2.3, §2.1.5 untouched — upheld.** §2.1 item 5 binds quit/teardown/hide semantics and your change touches none of them. One note: the *producer* half (retaining a cause in the lifecycle owner) is arguably §2.1 territory, and only lives comfortably in §2.3 because that is where the capability it answers is defined. Fine as drafted — but if §2.1 ever grows a state-observability clause, the producer half migrates there and §2.3 keeps only the transport.

### Binding changes before the successor PR opens

**(a) An owned-child fault must outrank a window-scoped cause. The code already says so.**

`settleBrainBoot` is `up && !brainFaulted`, and its JSDoc states the intent outright — *"Settles asynchronous Brain boot without erasing an owned-child fault that arrived while readiness was still resolving."* So the existing design **already encodes child-fault primacy**: a `brainFaulted` flag deliberately survives a would-be-successful boot.

Unqualified first-cause-wins contradicts that. Reachable sequence: cockpit `render-process-gone` latches `cause: render-process-gone`, then the Brain child actually dies. One bounded record, first cause wins → the diagnosis reports a **renderer crash while the organism is headless**. That is not the root fault; it is the lesser one arriving first.

Your cascade argument against last-cause-wins is correct and I am not asking you to invert it. Narrow it instead: **first cause wins within a severity tier, and an owned-child termination supersedes a window-scoped cause.** Still ONE bounded record, still never accumulated, and now consistent with `brainFaulted`'s existing primacy.

**(b) The consumer must re-read, not merely be permitted to.**

*"An unpolled or unanswerable affordance claims nothing"* is the right fail-closed shape for the channel — but as written the amendment **permits** pull without **obliging** re-reading. A leaf that calls `brainHealth()` once at mount satisfies the text literally and renders a permanently stale banner that looks authoritative. That is silence-reads-as-health arriving through the consumer instead of the channel — the exact failure you designed pull to prevent.

Bind the obligation, not the number: the consuming leaf owns a re-read cadence and a stale-read is not a health claim. Do **not** put an interval in the ADR — that is leaf property.

**(c) A cause must not survive into `stopped`.**

Your rule clears the cause on re-entry to `running`. `setBrainState('stopped')` fires on the quit/teardown paths (`appLifecycle.mjs:218`, `:247`), which never pass through `running` — so a degrade cause retained earlier in the session is still attached when the state is `stopped`. A consumer would render a clean explicit shutdown as impaired. Clear on `running` **and** on an explicit-quit `stopped`; an operator-initiated stop is not a fault.

### One note, not a change

The **witness obligation** is a testing discipline (drive a real transition; hand-assigned reducer state witnesses a pass-through), and I agree with it. But a shell-transition-driven witness over a *pull* transport has to tolerate one poll cycle — otherwise the leaf's E2E test is a race by construction. Worth one clause in the leaf's spec, not in the ADR.
