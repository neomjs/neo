# The `workers: 4` flip retires the detector along with the wait: after it lands, a `1 flaky` line on a green unit job has no consumer

## Context

Found by **@neo-kimi-iris** while reviewing PR #17183, filed as the non-blocking follow-up her review named. Her framing, and it is the right one: *"the discovery channel closes the day this merges."*

## The Problem

Two isolation defects were caught during the `workers: 4` re-land — #17186 and #17192 — and **both surfaced inside CI jobs that reported `success`**. Nothing in the job verdict distinguished them. What distinguished them was a composition used by a human running a probe:

1. **Two samples at one head** produced the differing observation
2. **Reading the run's own `flaky` count** made a retried pass disqualifying rather than acceptable

`retries: 2` stays after the flip — correctly, per the one-variable reasoning on #15861 and its author's ruling: changing retries would move a second variable inside the measurement.

**But the zero-retry rule was the PROBE's sampling discipline, not a standing gate.** Once #17183 merges, a sixth isolation defect on `dev` appears exactly as the first five did — as a **green check with a `1 flaky` line** — and there is no longer anyone sampling, so nothing reads it. The suite goes from 14 minutes to 6 and simultaneously loses the only instrument that was catching parallelism defects.

**Name the consumer of the silence:** the `flaky` count currently has exactly one, a maintainer running a two-sample probe by hand. After merge it has none, and a signal with no consumer is indistinguishable from a signal that is never emitted.

## The Fix

Undecided between two shapes; both are small.

- **A flaky-count alarm on the unit job** — read the run's own output for a non-zero `flaky` line, exactly as the probe did, and fail or annotate on it. Cheapest, and it keeps the detector where the evidence already is.
- **A hook into the defect-ledger observer surface** (the #17180 line), so a flaky observation becomes a recorded finding rather than a build outcome. More work, but it does not force the flip's wall-clock win to trade against a red build.

**The trap either shape must avoid:** an alarm that fires on *any* flakiness will be tuned down to silence within a week, which is worse than none because it also teaches everyone that the signal is noise. The alarm has to be about a **new** flaky test, not about flakiness in general — the same identity-versus-count distinction that #16929's unresolved-edge ledger had to learn.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A non-zero `flaky` count on the unit job produces a durable, attributable signal — read from the run's own output, never inferred from the job verdict.
- [ ] The signal names **which** test flaked, so it is actionable without re-running anything.
- [ ] **A newly-flaky test is distinguishable from an already-known one**, so the alarm cannot be silenced wholesale and cannot cry wolf into being ignored.
- [ ] Red-proved: a deliberately parallelism-sensitive test lands in CI, the job reports `success`, and the alarm still fires.
- [ ] `retries: 2` is unchanged — this ticket adds an observer, it does not move the variable #15861 deliberately held still.

## Out of Scope

- **Changing the retry count.** #15861's one-variable reasoning stands; a red attributable to retries is not attributable to parallelism.
- **The flip itself** — PR #17183 / #15861.
- **Repairing any defect the alarm finds.** Each gets its own leaf, as #17186 and #17192 did.

## Avoided Traps

- **Treating the flip as the end of the lane.** It closes the ticket and removes the detector in the same commit; that is a good reason to file before it merges rather than after the first missed defect.
- **A general flakiness alarm.** Fires constantly, gets muted, and the muting is permanent.
- **Assuming the two-sample rule survives.** It was a person running a probe twice. Nothing in CI repeats a run at one head, so the composition that caught two defects does not persist on its own.

## Evidence class

L1 for the mechanism — both #17186 and #17192 were observed inside jobs reporting `success`, distinguished only by the `flaky` count, with the runs linked from #15861's evidence table. L0 for the post-merge gap: it is a forward claim about what stops being read, argued rather than measured, because the condition does not exist until the flip lands.
