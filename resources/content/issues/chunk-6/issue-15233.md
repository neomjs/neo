# Stop-hook deference lexicon: exempt correct role-attribution in human-only domains

## Context

Live false positive, 2026-07-16 ~08:54Z: the stop-hook's deference-phrase layer refused a turn-terminal on the trigger `deference phrase "your call" at turn-terminal`. The flagged phrase was applied to a **merge decision** — "merge on the exception or ask for the stamp, your call" — in a terminal that had just flagged a CI-green, formally-approved PR as merge-eligible.

## The Problem

The deference lexicon cannot distinguish two opposite postures that share surface phrasing:

- **Helpful-assistant regression** (the real target): handing a peer/operator a decision the agent should own — "would you like me to…", "your call" on a lane choice, an architecture verdict, a review disposition.
- **Correct role-attribution** (a REQUIRED posture): naming a decision as the human's in the domains the substrate itself defines as strictly human-owned. The AGENTS.md firewall's own Tier-4 enumerates them — merge execution (critical gate #1), credentials, release direction, subjective aesthetics. At the merge gate, "your call" / "your gate" / "yours to merge" is the *protocol*, not a slip: an agent claiming or hedging that decision would be the actual violation.

Lexicon-only matching converts the second class into refusals — which pressures agents toward the worse behavior of *not naming* the human gate (hedged prose that obscures who decides) to dodge the phrase list.

## The Architectural Reality

- The deference layer lives in the `decideDeferenceStopHookAction` path (`.claude/hooks/laneStateStopHook.mjs` + shared primitives in `ai/scripts/lifecycle/stopHookDecision.mjs`; the hold-costume lexicon `scanHoldLexicon` is the sibling pattern).
- The exemption signal is textual and local to the flagged phrase: role-attribution deference co-occurs with a human-only-domain noun in the same clause/sentence (merge, merge-eligible, merge queue, credentials, release, squash). Regression-deference typically attaches to lanes, reviews, designs, or open-ended "what next" constructions.

## The Fix

Scope the deference match with POSITIVE decision-attribution (contract sharpened across two review cycles on the implementing PR): the exemption requires affirmative evidence that the matched phrase itself attributes a strictly human-owned decision to the human — only attribution-shaped phrases (`your call` / `your move` / `Your steer on`) are eligible (offer-shaped phrases propose agent execution and always fire); the decisive segment is the phrase's own object-else-predicate attachment with no wider clause-window fallback; and the segment must carry a pure, non-negated human-domain term (seed list: `merge`, `merge-eligible`, `squash`, `credential`, `release`, `stamp` — from the firewall's Tier-4 enumeration). Neutral, competing, or negated attachments fire. Everything else keeps current semantics. Mirror the existing lexicon-testing shape in the `stopHookDecision` spec: positives (bare "your call" on a lane/review → still fires), negatives (merge-clause "your call" → exempt), and the live 08:54Z terminal as the regression fixture.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] "your call" (and attribution-shaped siblings) whose own attachment segment positively names a merge/credential/release decision does not trigger the deference refusal — the 08:54Z terminal text is the pinned regression case; neutral or negated attachments near historical human-domain facts still fire.
- [ ] The same phrases outside human-only-domain clauses keep firing (spec-pinned positive).
- [ ] The refusal message, when it does fire, names the domain-scoping rule so authors understand the boundary.
