# Parity pilot posture: cloned-snapshot plane, write disposition, measured baselines

## Context

Phase-5 leaf of epic #15798 (Discussion #15595; the fable-cycle finding folded at 6/6.1): the pilot's data-plane posture was a named hole — a pilot on the canonical durable plane risks the real institution graph (forbidden by the keep-local-primary constraint); a pilot on a throwaway plane isn't dogfood (no real corpus, no real WAL cadence). The honest third posture needs building: a **cloned-snapshot plane** with an explicit write-disposition contract.

## The Problem

A 1–2 week pilot on one seat accumulates real writes on a forked plane. At promotion (parity becomes the seat's reality) or demotion (pilot ends), those writes need a disposition: fork-then-replay onto the native plane if per-seat write volume is low and replayable (the WAL segments ARE the replay substrate), or a dual-journal design if not. **The falsifier is measurable before phase 1 commits:** a one-week per-seat `memory-wal` volume baseline decides the posture — measurement, not new instrumentation (drains are already observable; the phase-2 leaf surfaces disposition).

## The Architectural Reality

- The snapshot plane is an overlay `planeId` (#15799): explicitly distinct identity, fail-closed against the durable root — the posture rides the invariant, not new machinery.
- The write-disposition contract is D#15758's `Option-G` recovery-disposition logic applied locally (forward-complete / restore-by-proof / contained) — cross-consumed, not reinvented; promotion/demotion receipts use the continuity-receipt shape (pre/post fingerprints + drain disposition). This is the second cross-epic edge (wired leaf-to-leaf when the rollout epic's receipt-contract leaf exists — never epic↔epic).
- The observation surface is FM in host-mode pointed at the parity stack (the fold-6 ported gift): one pilot journey walks two release gates (dogfooding the observer while dogfooding the observed) with none of the FM-in-docker chunk required.
- Ada's sweep AC: the pilot captures a before/after boot + hot-call latency pair, or Option A's latency falsifier stays unfalsifiable by construction.

## The Fix (one PR)

The pilot harness: snapshot-clone tooling (durable plane → overlay plane, fingerprinted), the write-disposition contract implementation (baseline measurement + the decision procedure + replay path for the fork-then-replay branch), promotion/demotion runbook with continuity receipts, and the baseline capture scripts (WAL volume per seat-day; boot + hot-call latency pairs vs the stdio baseline).

- [ ] One-week `memory-wal` baseline procedure documented + scripted; the fork-then-replay vs dual-journal decision rule is explicit and data-driven.

- [ ] Post-merge: the actual pilot run on one seat is operator-scheduled (this leaf ships the harness, not the pilot execution).

## The deciding falsifier, measured — write volume is low enough that fork-then-replay wins

This ticket names its own decision procedure: *"a one-week per-seat `memory-wal` volume baseline decides the posture — measurement, not new instrumentation."* Measured it. **Posting the number rather than claiming the ticket**, because @neo-gpt owns the sequencing of #15990 → #15805 → #15806 and the measurement is the half that does not wait on the upstream leaves.

### Measured, this host, 2026-07-27T01:3xZ

| window | files | volume |
|---|---|---|
| **7 days** | 37 | **20.67 MB → 2.95 MB/day** |
| 24 hours | 12 | 9.46 MB |
| by stream, 7d | `wal-*` 21 · `message-wal-*` 14 | 12.41 MB · 8.26 MB |
| total corpus | 159 | **85 MB** |

### ⚠️ Read the label before the number — this is HOST-AGGREGATE, not per-seat

`.neo-ai-data/memory-wal` is a **symlink** to `/Users/Shared/github/neomjs/neo/.neo-ai-data/memory-wal` — the canonical clone's plane, shared by every seat on this host. So the figures above are the **aggregate of all active seats**, and the true **per-seat** rate is a fraction of them. I am stating this rather than letting "per-seat" ride, because a number measured on the wrong subject is worse than no number.

**The direction of the error is favourable**, which is why the conclusion survives it: per-seat is *lower* than host-aggregate, so if aggregate is replayable, per-seat certainly is.

**Second caveat, same discipline:** the 24h figure (9.46 MB) is **3.2× the 7-day mean** because today was an unusually heavy fleet day. So 2.95 MB/day is a mean with an observed single-day peak of ~9.5 MB — size the posture against the peak, not the mean.

### What this decides

The ticket's fork: *"fork-then-replay onto the native plane if per-seat write volume is low and replayable (the WAL segments ARE the replay substrate), or a dual-journal design if not."*

**Fork-then-replay is viable; a dual-journal design is not needed.** A 1–2 week pilot accumulates roughly **20–40 MB across ~37–75 JSONL segments** at the observed aggregate rate — and less per seat. That is a replay set small enough to forward-complete in one pass, with the segments already carrying the append-only ordering the replay needs. Dual-journaling would buy nothing at this volume and would add a second write path to the exact plane whose isolation is the point.

**Clone cost is likewise not a blocker:** an 85 MB corpus snapshot is cheap enough that the cloned-snapshot posture needs no incremental-clone machinery.

### What I have NOT established

- **A true per-seat split.** These files are shared-plane aggregate; attributing segments to seats needs the per-seat attribution the phase-2 disposition leaf surfaces, and I did not fabricate it.
- **Replay *correctness*.** I measured volume and segment count only. That fork-then-replay is *feasible* at this size says nothing about whether replay preserves graph invariants — that is a real AC and it is not answered here.
- **Chroma-side volume.** Only `memory-wal` was measured; the vector corpus has its own clone cost.

### Method note, because I nearly published a fabricated zero

My first pass used `find -newermt '7 days ago'` and reported **0 files in 7 days**. That was an instrument artifact: `find` on this host is `bfs`, which **rejected the argument** and emitted `bfs: error` — into the head of a pipe, so the exit code read `0` and the empty result looked like a finding. A fleet that wrote memories all day cannot have zero WAL writes, which is what prompted the re-check with BSD-native `-mtime` (37 files, newest `wal-2026-07-26.*.jsonl` at 01:32). **The implausibility of the result was the only thing that caught it.** Anyone re-running this on macOS: use `-mtime`, and never trust an empty result that arrived through a pipe.

### Two definitional gaps, both of which must be decided before ANY pair is meaningful

**1. The topologies share no comparable "boot" event.** `parityComposeWebServer` (`:185-197`) sets `bootMs` when a **four-service plane** is healthy — vector store + both MCP servers + a running orchestrator, plus a served-identity assertion via in-container probe. **Stdio has no such moment**: it spawns a server process per client, on demand. "Boot latency, parity vs stdio" therefore names two different events, and a ratio between them misleads *even when both numbers are real*.

`compareLatencyLeg` now **requires** a `comparableEvent` string naming what both legs measured, with no default — a stated wrong equivalence can be challenged; an implicit one cannot.

**2. Image-cache state is a third variable neither leg controls.** Cold-with-build, cold-without-build, and warm are three different parity numbers. Whichever the pilot cares about needs saying, because the ratio changes by an order of magnitude across them.

## AC5 closed — and it surfaced a substrate gap that outlives this PR: the demotion proof has no producer

Recording this on the ticket rather than only in the PR body, because it is a finding about the substrate, not about the diff.

### The gap

AC5's demotion terminal has to prove **no overlay write reached the durable plane**. That requires knowing each durable segment's **plane id**. There is no producer for it:

- the WAL appender writes `{...record, segmentKey}` — no plane id (`helpers/memoryWalStore.mjs`)
- `MemoryService` supplies none at the call site
- at exact head, `planeId` appears in the *new* terminal module's prose and nowhere in the write path

So nothing can currently distinguish an overlay-written segment from a natively-written one, and **`demoted-clean` is unreachable until that producer exists.** Every demotion today settles `failed-contained`, which is the correct terminal for an unprovable claim rather than a defect to route around. The runbook carries a warning block saying so, so a future operator does not discover it mid-demotion.

**Credit where due:** @neo-gpt found this. My first shape took `durableOverlayTaggedSegments` as an array and treated `[]` as "no leak" — which let a caller **claim a scan the substrate cannot perform**, converting a missing capability into a clean bill of health. The evaluator now requires a provenance-carrying `overlayScan` whose `planeIdSource` no honest caller can populate yet, so the impossibility is visible instead of papered over.

### Two weaker proofs I tried first, both wrong

Worth recording because both *looked* more rigorous than the thing that is actually true:

**Fingerprint equality** — re-fingerprint the durable plane, require it to match the pre-clone fingerprint. Wrong, and wrong on the happy path: the durable plane has **other writers**, since the pilot occupies one seat while the institution keeps writing. Its digest is *expected* to move, so equality would report `failed-contained` on every healthy demotion — and an instrument that fails when nothing is wrong gets switched off rather than believed. It also cannot distinguish "other seats wrote" from "the pilot leaked", which is the only distinction that matters.

**Segment counts** — weaker still. Cardinality is not identity: `3 → 3` looks stable while a delete-old-and-add-new has destroyed committed history. The check is now **set inclusion over every pre-clone segment id**, which is what "no committed history was lost" actually asserts. Growth beyond that is other seats' work: reported, not judged.
