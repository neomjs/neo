# CSS-layer flicker taxonomy: style-delta frame-split and dock add/remove churn

## Context

Seeded by the operator after take-15's verdict arc (2026-07-25/26): the projection-transaction body-clear class got its fix (#15951) and its instruments (#15950 census, the CDP entropy witness), and the natural next question is the **CSS layer of the flicker taxonomy**. This ticket is deliberately framed as *enumeration-first*: two candidate classes are named by hypothesis, to be verified or falsified with per-class repro witnesses — never asserted from appearance.

## The Problem (hypothesis, unverified)

Two candidate classes, both plausible from the week's projection work:

1. **Style-delta frame-split** — a style change whose deltas don't land in the same frame as the structural change they belong to (e.g. the flex application in the stable-topology path, `setSilent` + host update, overflow re-projection timing). The presentation shows one frame of the old style against the new structure.
2. **Dock layout add/remove churn** — any path that destroys and re-creates chrome or panes (the staged transaction by design, pane add/remove, overflow menu open/close) presenting intermediate states the eye reads as flicker even when every individual frame is "correct."

Neither is established. The take-15 reel (87MB, `take15-001227-d2.mov` in the film checkout) is a ready corpus to census for both.

## The Architectural Reality

- The stable-topology fix (#15951) eliminated the shell-swap class at the *projection* layer; anything remaining lives at the style-delta or add/remove layers.
- Instruments that make this falsifiable now exist: the CDP presented-frame entropy witness (per-action continuity, in-spec) and the frame-delta flicker census (`test/playwright/util/flickerCensus.mjs` — any video file, with timestamps).

## The Fix (enumeration-first)

1. Per-class repro witnesses on the take-15 reel (or a fresh take if the reel is too clean post-#15951): census, frame-pair the deltas against the beat log, and classify each anomaly into class 1, class 2, or neither.

3. A falsification record for any candidate that does NOT reproduce — stated, not dropped quietly.

### Class 1 — style-delta frame-split: excursions found, and they are the pacing gap, not a product defect

d2 carries eight luma steps of **±35 to ±40 units** (on a 0–255 scale), several reverting within **1–3 frames** — at 60 fps that is a **16–50 ms** excursion:

```
t= 5.417s   84.65 -> 121.31   (+36.67)   reverts after 2 frames
t= 7.000s   84.57 -> 124.62   (+40.05)   reverts after 3 frames
t= 8.883s   86.73 -> 121.67   (+34.94)   reverts after 1 frame
t=11.283s   84.46 -> 124.46   (+40.00)
t=14.083s   85.24 -> 122.96   (+37.73)
t=14.568s  124.54 ->  84.13   (-40.42)
```

The 84 ↔ 124 endpoints are stable and repeated, which is a **theme toggle** (light ↔ dark), not stochastic flicker. Sub-50ms reverts on a theme beat is precisely the class @neo-gpt-emmy already characterised on the film-v1 smoke corpus: *"the scene-1 theme beat at 80/120ms receives zero readable light frames — a film pacing gap (FilmPace covers gestures only), not a product event."*

**So corpus 2 reproduces corpus 1's verdict rather than contradicting it.** Two independent corpora, same conclusion: what looks like style-delta flicker is the theme beat running faster than the recording can render readably, and `FilmPace` not covering theme transitions is the mechanism. That is a **film-pacing** fix, not a CSS-layer fix.

### A third observation, unhypothesised and worth its own look

**d1 is constant at 113.9 luma across all 241 frames** — zero variation, max frame-to-frame delta 0.02. The vessel stream contains no dynamic content whatsoever.

The spec log corroborates a cause: `[vessel-probe] popup created urlAtOpen=about:blank` / `popup loaded about:blank` / `urlAtLoad":"about:blank"`. The vessel window recorded a blank document for its entire 19.6s.

I am **not** asserting the vessel is broken — scene 2 passed including its back-IN morph witness, and a vessel shell may legitimately begin at `about:blank` and receive content through the worker rather than by navigation. But the pairing (blank `urlAtLoad` **and** a perfectly flat luma trace) is discriminating enough that **any film assembled from this reel's d1 stream shows an empty second window**, and someone should establish which of those two readings is true before the reel is used as film source. Flagging rather than diagnosing: it is adjacent to #15252 and I have not claimed that.

### Instrument caveats, because two of them nearly produced wrong answers

1. **`r_frame_rate` is a container lie for d1.** It reports `240/1`, but 241 frames over 19.60s is **~12.3 fps actual** — a ~20× error. Any census that timestamps excursions from nominal fps mis-places every d1 event. I used real `pts_time`. d1 also emits non-monotonic DTS warnings on decode, consistent with an irregular capture rate.
2. **Robust z-scores are degenerate on this material and must not be reported.** Because both streams are near-static frame-to-frame (median |Δ| = 0.001, MAD = 0.001), the z denominator collapses: real theme steps score z ≈ 50,000, while d1's **0.02-unit noise** still scores z ≈ 22. Ranking by z alone would have presented d1's noise floor as "excursions with z=22." **Absolute magnitude is the only honest discriminator here** — d1's largest step is 0.02 luma units, which is nothing. I report the distribution and the top-N by magnitude rather than applying a tuned threshold, because on this data a threshold chooses the conclusion.
3. Luma was measured at `scale=320` — frame-average luma is scale-invariant for flash detection, but a **spatially localised** flash (one small panel flashing) is diluted by full-frame averaging. So class 1's result is sound for full-frame theme events and is **not** a complete probe for a small-region flicker. That is a real gap in my instrument and a per-region census would close it.
